SLIDE 1: TITLE
==================================================================
On the slide:
Taxipörssi
The taxi marketplace where offers set the price.
The algorithm suggests, the driver offers, the customer chooses.

Speaker notes:
Taxipörssi ("taxi exchange") is a two-sided marketplace for taxi rides. Big ride-hailing platforms set
prices from above. Here the price forms the way it does on an exchange: an algorithm suggests a fair
price, independent drivers make offers, and the customer picks the offer that suits them best.


==================================================================
SLIDE 2: THE PROBLEM
==================================================================
On the slide:
- Drivers are entrepreneurs, but the platform sets their price
- Large platforms take a big share of every fare [check current commission levels]
- Customers see one price and can't tell how it was set
- Surge pricing feels arbitrary to both sides

Speaker notes:
Most taxi drivers are self-employed, yet on the big platforms they can't influence the price of their
own work. The platform decides the price and the commission, and the driver can only accept or decline.
The customer gets one number with no comparison and no explanation. Nobody on either side feels in
control of the price.


==================================================================
SLIDE 3: THE IDEA
==================================================================
On the slide:
A price exchange for taxi rides
1. The algorithm suggests a fair price from live market data
2. Drivers accept the suggestion or make a counter-offer
3. The customer compares offers and chooses
4. After the ride, ratings reward good drivers with lower fees

Speaker notes:
The algorithm doesn't dictate the price, it anchors it. Drivers can go above or below the suggestion,
for example charge a little more at rush hour or less when it's quiet. The customer sees real offers
side by side (price, pickup time, rating, car) and decides. Supply and demand meet openly, which is why
the product is called a "pörssi", an exchange.


==================================================================
SLIDE 4: HOW A RIDE WORKS
==================================================================
On the slide:
Customer: picks a destination, sees the estimated price, taps "Request offers"
Driver: gets the request, offers the suggested price or their own
Customer: sees offers arrive, picks one, follows the car on the map
Both: the ride ends, the customer rates, and the driver's profile updates

Speaker notes:
This is exactly the flow in the demo. The request reaches the driver within seconds, and the driver has
30 seconds to respond. The customer's choice decides what the driver sees next: either "The customer
accepted your offer!" or "The customer chose another driver", along with the winning price and pickup
time so the driver learns from it. The agreed price is fixed before the ride starts.

SLIDE 5: THE DRIVER APP
==================================================================
On the slide:
- Market view: price per km over the last 1 h / 4 h / 24 h
- "Good time to drive": open requests vs. free cars, plus a price forecast
- One-screen offer: suggested price, ±€1 buttons, or type your own price
- Live acceptance probability as you change the price
- Earnings: today's total and extra earned from counter-offers

Speaker notes:
The driver app is built for use in traffic: dark night mode, large buttons, high contrast. The market
chart works like a stock chart and helps the driver decide whether to start driving now. When a request
arrives, the driver sees the pickup distance, trip length, customer rating and the suggested price, and
can adjust it with one thumb. An acceptance-probability bar shows the trade-off between a higher price
and the risk of losing the ride.

In the demo: all of the above, with simulated market data.


==================================================================
SLIDE 6: THE CUSTOMER APP
==================================================================
On the slide:
- Pick a destination and see an estimated price immediately
- Get several offers: price, pickup time, rating, car
- Choose the offer that fits: cheapest, fastest or best rated
- Follow the car on a live map, with the license plate shown
- Rate the driver after the ride

Speaker notes:
The customer gets choice and transparency. Before ordering, the customer sees an estimate and the usual
price range for the trip. Offers then arrive one by one, and the customer decides what matters most:
price, speed or quality. The customer doesn't see how each offer compares with the estimate, only the
offer itself, so the choice stays simple.

In the demo: the full flow on a real map of central Helsinki, with routes along real streets.


==================================================================
SLIDE 7: THE PRICE ENGINE
==================================================================
On the slide:
- Live market price per km, updated continuously
- Suggested price = base fare + distance × current market price
- A price band shows where similar rides are agreed right now
- Acceptance model: how likely a customer is to accept a given price

Speaker notes:
In the demo the engine is a transparent formula. The market price reacts to time of day and demand,
e.g. a Friday night rush. The suggestion anchors the negotiation so offers stay reasonable, and drivers
can only bid within a set range around it (±30% in the demo), which prevents extreme prices.

Possible next: a machine-learning model trained on real supply and demand, time, weather, events
(concerts, ferry arrivals, flights) and historical acceptance rates.

SLIDE 8: RATINGS, TIERS AND FEES
==================================================================
On the slide:
Better ratings = lower fees
- Bronze: 10% service fee
- Silver: 9%
- Gold: 8%
- Platinum: 6%
Tiers depend on average rating and number of rides driven.

Speaker notes:
Quality pays off directly. Every customer rating feeds the driver's average, and the average and ride
count set the tier. A Platinum driver pays 6% instead of 10%. In the demo that saves the driver
€142.30 a month. Platinum drivers also get priority on pre-booked rides such as airport and business
trips, and their offers are listed first. If the average drops below the tier threshold, the driver
moves down a tier and the fee goes up, which the app shows clearly.

In the demo: rating distribution, reviews, tier card with the fee highlighted, and a new rating from
the customer app appears in the driver's profile right away.

SLIDE 9: BUSINESS MODEL
==================================================================
On the slide:
- Service fee of 6–10% per ride, depending on the driver's tier
- Lower than typical platform commissions [check and cite current figures]
- Volume grows with liquidity: more drivers, more offers, faster matches

Possible additional revenue:
- Business accounts with monthly invoicing
- Pre-booked rides (airport, events) with a booking fee
- Market data and analytics for fleet owners

Speaker notes:
The model is simple: a percentage of each completed ride. The fee is deliberately lower than what drivers
pay on the big platforms, which is the main reason for drivers to switch. The fee falls as quality rises,
so the platform earns a little less per ride from its best drivers but keeps them loyal. Add-on revenue
can come from business customers and pre-booked rides.

SLIDE 10: WHY DRIVERS JOIN
==================================================================
On the slide:
- Set your own price, like a real entrepreneur
- Lower fees: 6–10%
- Market data that shows when driving pays best
- Honest feedback: see why you lost a ride
- Good service is rewarded with a lower fee

Speaker notes:
Drivers are the scarce side of the market, so the pitch is built around them. Taxipörssi gives back
control over price and shows the data needed to use it well. When a customer picks another driver, the
app shows the winning price and pickup time, so the driver can adjust next time.


==================================================================
SLIDE 11: WHY CUSTOMERS USE IT
==================================================================
On the slide:
- Several real offers instead of one take-it-or-leave-it price
- Choose by price, pickup time or rating
- The price is fixed before the ride starts
- Better-rated drivers are easy to find

Speaker notes:
For the customer, Taxipörssi is like comparing flight prices, but in seconds. Competition between
drivers keeps prices fair, and ratings make quality visible. The estimate shown up front helps the
customer judge whether an offer is reasonable.

SLIDE 12: COMPETITION AND DIFFERENTIATION
==================================================================
On the slide:
- Global platforms (e.g. Uber, Bolt): the platform sets the price
- Local taxi dispatch apps: [list local competitors in the target city]
- Bidding apps (e.g. inDrive): the passenger proposes a price
- Taxipörssi: an algorithm anchors the price, drivers compete, quality lowers fees

Speaker notes:
Bidding for rides already exists. For example, in inDrive the passenger proposes a price and drivers
respond [check the current model]. Taxipörssi differs in three ways:
1) an algorithmic suggestion anchors every negotiation, so prices stay fair and offers are fast;
2) drivers get market data (price chart, demand) to price well;
3) the tier system ties customer ratings directly to the driver's fee.
It's built for markets where many taxi drivers are independent entrepreneurs.

SLIDE 14: THE DEMO
==================================================================
On the slide:
Interactive demo: customer app and driver app side by side
[Link to the demo]

Suggested demo script (about 3–4 minutes):
1. Driver: show the market chart (1 h / 4 h / 24 h) and "Good time to drive"
2. Customer: pick "Home", show the estimate, tap "Request offers"
3. Driver: the request appears; raise the price by €1 and tap "Offer"
4. Customer: offers arrive, and Mikko's (Platinum) is listed first; choose it
5. Driver: "The customer accepted your offer!"; tap "Start pickup"
6. Both phones follow the same ride: the ETA counts down, then "Ride complete"

the other side is waiting for action. All data in the demo is simulated. The map and routes use real
Helsinki streets (OpenStreetMap).

SLIDE 16: RISKS AND HOW WE ANSWER THEM
==================================================================
On the slide:
- Liquidity: enough drivers and customers at the same time
  -> start in one city, onboard drivers first with the low fee
- Speed: customers want a car now, not an auction
  -> offers arrive in seconds; the suggestion makes accepting quick
- Race to the bottom on price
  -> bids are limited to a range around the suggestion; ratings reward quality, not just low price
- Regulation and trust
  -> licence verification, fixed prices agreed up front, ratings

Speaker notes:
The biggest risk for any marketplace is liquidity. The plan is to win drivers first, because they are
the scarce side and they benefit most from lower fees and pricing control.
